Minjerribah · North Stradbroke Island · Quandamooka Country

Two working models of one island, and what they are for.

The Straddie Twins are digital twins of Minjerribah that run in a web browser from public records: the tide, the boats, the roads, the aquifer, the council levers, the people. They exist to work out how a place carries itself through every existential threat, known or imagined, and stays somewhere people want to be. This site walks both, part by part, with nothing assumed.

In one screen

The island is the first rung, not the point.

The programme these twins belong to describes itself as one ladder from the Gumpi jetty to a civilisation that keeps the lights on through anything, held inside joyful responsible abundance so that people actually want to live through it. The island is where that ladder starts, at a scale a person can hold whole.

A twin is the instrument for it. Write down what a place has published about itself: the ferry timetable, the road network, the tide tables, the freshwater lens, the council's own list of decisions. Put a clock on it. Let the day run. Then push it, and see which answers hold weight and which were guesses wearing a number.

Two have been built for Minjerribah. The first is a living world you can fly around, with residents, whales, a barge and a council in tray. The second started again from the instruments up, and its worked example is the crossing: one question driven all the way to an answer you can check.

Minjerribah is Quandamooka Country. Both twins acknowledge the Quandamooka People as the Traditional Owners of Minjerribah and of the lands and waters of Moreton Bay, and pay respect to Elders past and present. They are simulations built from public records and carry no cultural material.
