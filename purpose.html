Close to home

Six reasons this matters

Your tap. The mainland has been drinking this island since 1996. Eight Olympic pools a day, one every three hours. The island itself uses two thirds of one.

Your power bill. Nobody has measured the swell out the front. One estimate puts half a kilometre of it at six times what the whole island gets through, day and night, rain or shine.

The car queue. $41 million is committed to the new terminal, staged over years. It still does not say where you park. About 375,000 people visited in 2018 against 2,200 residents, and in peak weeks the population triples.

Amity Point. It is losing ground to the sea. Houses, the road, the rock wall.

When the barge stops. The shelves empty. Same as any town on one road.

Work. Young people leave the island for jobs that could be here.

Ranked first

The solar micro-nova

A micro-nova is a sudden burst of energy off the sun, big enough to wreck the grid. The founder ranks it first.

Snowball Earth, the planet freezing over, tests it hardest. A civilisation that can "live warm under the sand on banked energy while the surface freezes" has answered every smaller cold snap for free.

Everything moves together

A document argues. A model runs.

Tide, weather, the fresh water, the sand core, the dug-out sand and the power load move together, or the answer is wrong. The first build calls it rehearsal: plans are "tested as simulations before anyone spends money, goodwill or Country on them".

It survives the room

Anyone can check the model

A block at Amity Point needs rezoning. Rezoning means the government changes what the land can be used for, and it is slow and political. The strongest thing to bring is "a model anyone can check". Across the whole build, "claims become runnable models anyone can re-run".

It gets ahead of the law

Model now, build when the law changes

Federal law bans nuclear power here. Thorium is a reactor fuel instead of uranium. "A Straddie student can build a virtual thorium reactor now, stress-test it against real physics, and be years ahead on the day the law changes."

The island owns it

The island can say no

MODFLOW is free groundwater software that works out how water moves through sand. An open MODFLOW twin of the lens means "the community ends up owning the model that any future decision depends on".

Responsibility plus abundance, no joy

The well-run warehouse

"Everything accounted for, nothing worth accounting for." A place run like that is safe until everyone quietly leaves.

A shelter that keeps people breathing and gives them nothing to stay for empties out. The plan asks "how people live well, not merely survive, during stress".

A sand-sports ground and a cinema sit in the tunnel corridor: "somewhere for a Saturday and a film night".

Power here is "a responsible harvest, everywhere, gradually, for generations". Minerals and materials stay for what the island needs, not for export or revenue.

One line in the files covers it: "enough, made well, made yours, made to last, and nothing thrown away that another process wanted."
