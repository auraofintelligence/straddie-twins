Terminal to city

The chain runs from a car park to a closed loop.

It begins with a ferry terminal being rebuilt right now that does not solve where the cars go, and ends with a subterranean eco-city. Every link is a quantity the twin has to carry. These are the numbers the repositories already fix.

Happening now

The Gumpi terminal, and the question it leaves open.

The Dunwich (Gumpi) ferry terminal upgrade at Junner Street is funded and under way: $41 million in total, $10 million from the Australian Government, $30 million from the Queensland Government and $1 million from local government. It is one of 29 South East Queensland City Deal commitments, and concept-design consultation ran from 28 May to 21 June 2026.

The terminal is the island's handshake with the mainland. What the upgrade does not resolve is where vehicles wait, which the repositories carry as a pressure measured in decades rather than seasons, and as a modelling lane rather than a complaint.

Where do cars wait when the island is full? If parking has been tight for decades, could mainland shopper days, holiday peaks and resident access be modelled with park-and-ride nodes, autonomous shuttles or a tunnel-linked parking loop?

The site as it stands

A build pad, not a heritage frontage.

Every community design brief the terminal lab generates carries the same site reading: treat the current photo as a mostly blank, overgrown former sand-mining terminal site. It is a pad for future infrastructure.

The objective, corrected

Speed of tunnelling is not the problem. Holding the wet sand open is.

The second build carries the owner's own correction at the head of its ground physics file, dated 19 August 2026. It moves the objective function off the number most tunnelling literature optimises.

Speed of tunnelling doesnt matter to me. the tunnelling is arbitrary. the wall casting and energy to freeze the wet sand or hold the sand in place when all it wants to do is collapse is the objective.

That reorders everything downstream. Wall area rises with bore diameter while spoil rises with the square of it, so the bore is the single number that decides how much network a fixed amount of casting and ground support can buy. The freeze energy is computed from the physics rather than assumed: latent heat of fusion at 334 kilojoules a kilogram, water at 4.18, ice at 2.09 and quartz at 0.83 kilojoules per kilogram per kelvin, quartz at 2,650 kilograms a cubic metre.

The number that decides the method

Hydraulic conductivity, unmeasured.

Forty-two metres of head over about 5,500 metres gives a gradient of 0.0076. Seepage runs from 0.02 metres a day in very fine sand to 2.18 in gravel. A freeze is straightforward below about one metre a day, so conductivity decides the ground-support method and nothing else does.

It has not been measured on this island. The twin is where a measured value would flip the method.

Shallow works, not deep ones

Buoyancy is a portal problem.

At a 1.5 metre bore, 0.37 metres of cover holds the tunnel down. Uplift does not vary with depth and cover does, which makes buoyancy a constraint on portals and shallow loops. The park-and-ride loop is exactly that kind of work.

The legal wall

What the law permits, and where it has to move.

  • North Stradbroke Island Protection and Sustainability Act 2011. The Act that phased sand mining out sets the terms any ground disturbance is read against.
  • Native title, recognised 4 July 2011. Two Federal Court consent determinations recognise Quandamooka rights and interests. Sea Country agreement is written as a precondition on reef placement, not a consultation step after it.
  • Water. The Queensland Water Regulation Amendment 2023 impact analysis and the Seqwater drought response plan for the island govern the lens.
  • Zoning. On a 1.37 hectare block at Amity Point, the zoning permits one use, a place of worship, from a precinct plan written for another purpose.
  • EPBC Act 1999 and the Australian Radiation Protection and Nuclear Safety Act 1998. The federal prohibitions on nuclear installations. The response is to model now and be years ahead the day the law changes.
  • Radiological handling. Monazite is naturally radioactive, so separation means licensed handling, monitored workers and containment.
  • CASA. Every capture flight that feeds the twin's evidence runs to a pre-flight checklist of aviation rules and local permissions.
  • The live administrative gate. The terminal upgrade sits with Queensland Transport and Main Roads under the City Deal, and its feedback windows are dated.
The programme is written to work inside the law and to press it where it blocks the work, with a model as the argument. The pattern is the same each time: build the thing in simulation, publish it so anyone can re-run it, and arrive at the decision with numbers rather than a position.
