Terminal to city

From ferry ramp to underground city

The Dunwich ferry terminal is being rebuilt now. It still does not say where the cars go. The chain ends in a city under the sand.

Happening now

The terminal and the cars

$41 million is committed to the Dunwich (Gumpi) ferry terminal at Junner Street: $10 million federal, $30 million Queensland, $1 million local. It is staged across the build, not money sitting in an account today. One of 29 South East Queensland City Deal commitments. Consultation ran 28 May to 21 June 2026. No construction start date has been published.

The upgrade does not say where vehicles wait. Parking has been tight for decades.

About 375,000 visited in 2018. No newer count has reached the project. About 2,200 live here. That is 170 visitors for every resident, and it does not arrive evenly: in peak weeks the island's population triples. The civic pack files it under what angers beach users: carparks full by eight.

The objective, corrected

Holding the wet sand open

The second build opens its ground physics file with a correction, dated 19 August 2026.

Speed of tunnelling doesnt matter to me. the tunnelling is arbitrary.

Wall casting and freeze energy are what count. Wall area rises with the bore, spoil with the square of it. So the bore decides how much tunnel a casting budget buys. The freeze energy falls out of the physics: the heat it takes to freeze water, the heat water and ice and quartz each hold, and how much a cubic metre of quartz weighs. Run it and a cubic metre of wet sand costs about 55 units of power to freeze. That is 550 kettle boils, or three days of an ordinary house. It buys the ice once. Holding it is not costed anywhere, and the file says so.

The number that decides the method

Nobody has measured the seepage

The water sits 42 metres higher in the middle than at the coast, five and a half kilometres away. That is less than a centimetre of fall for every metre you walk, flatter than a wheelchair ramp. Through very fine sand the water creeps two centimetres a day. Through gravel, over two metres. The ground file puts the freeze limit near a metre a day.

Nobody has measured it here. A real value would flip the method.

Portals and shallow loops

Shallow tunnels want to float

At a 1.5 m bore, 0.37 m of cover holds the tunnel down. Uplift stays the same at any depth, cover does not. Floating bites at portals and shallow loops, the park-and-ride among them.

That 0.37 rests on a concrete lining and a submerged sand weight the file itself calls the figure to argue with, not a measurement of this island. Change the sand and the cover changes with it.

The law

The law as it stands

  • North Stradbroke Island Protection and Sustainability Act 2011. It ended sand mining and sets the terms for any digging.
  • Native title, recognised 4 July 2011. Two Federal Court consent determinations recognise Quandamooka rights and interests. Sea Country agreement comes before any reef.
  • Water. The Queensland Water Regulation Amendment 2023 impact analysis and the Seqwater drought response plan govern the lens.
  • Zoning. A 1.37 hectare block at Amity Point permits one use, a place of worship, from a plan written for something else.
  • EPBC Act 1999 and the Australian Radiation Protection and Nuclear Safety Act 1998. Federal law bans nuclear installations. Model it now, be years ahead the day the law changes.
  • Radiological handling. Monazite is naturally radioactive, so separating it means licensed handling, monitored workers and containment.
  • CASA. Every capture flight runs to a checklist of aviation rules and local permissions.
  • The live gate. Queensland Transport and Main Roads runs the upgrade under the City Deal. Its feedback windows are dated.
