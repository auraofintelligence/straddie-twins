running

It is in the build and it does its job.

A system that ticks, a layer that draws, a board that opens, a pack that loads, an instrument that checks. Where the build measures its own faults, those faults are listed with it.

partly

Some of it exists.

Four of fifteen connectors have a reader; one feed of four is read by the running twin; five fetchers exist and one pack from them is committed.

designed

It is written down and not written.

The design exists as a document or a contract; the code does not. Each is reported by the build itself rather than left to be discovered.

First build

What the first build has designed and not written.

  • Five modules named in its manifest as planned and reported at start-up: fire (danger, ignition, spread and track closures), coast (erosion and accretion at Amity Point), groundwater (the aquifer, the lakes, the borefield), shared presentation read models so boards would describe a day rather than an instant, and a stewardship board for the living island beside the civic one.
  • Scanned assets. The contract for how a real scanned object would arrive is written, down to the file names and what the twin would and would not trust from a scan. The folder is empty and no asset has been delivered.
  • Ten participation lanes, ordered from the lowest technology up: a spoken correction, paper cards, a tape measure, business and community facts, scans, sightings, wishes, scenario suggestions, memory anchors and event plans. The spoken correction has been used, and an intake tool turns a typed-up batch into records with attribution, consent, visibility and a coarsened location. There is no form, no portal and no booth software.
  • The era lane would let the island be looked at as it was. It is not built, because the build holds no georeferenced historical map and no historical aerial photography, and its documents say a gap beats a confident fabrication.
  • Scenario running. Only the stamp exists, the record that a scenario was run; no scenario has been stamped, and none could be while the gate was red.
  • The gate was red at the last commit, with twelve findings: mostly civic records without a source and stale checksums. Promotion of new records and scenario stamps stay blocked until a person resolves them.
  • A scheduled sync. Every connector states how often a person should run it and why; nothing runs on a schedule, so on a day nobody syncs, the weather feed is stale by its own rule and live mode reads "not known".

Second build

What the second build does not have on screen.

  • No map and no island geometry. The only thing the renderer draws is a calibration scene on the lab page: flat rectangles on purpose, so a program can check the picture to the pixel. A rendered coastline waits on the licence decision described on the data page, because the coastline data carries a share-alike condition.
  • No residents. The rule for disclosing a generated person on every card is written; no people exist to disclose.
  • No live feed of anything. The ferry page is the printed timetable read against the clock. In the build's own words, on the day the barge stops, the page would still show the barge as scheduled, and its board names that as the gap that matters most.
  • The live bus rung, TransLink's real-time vehicle positions above the printed timetable, has not been started.
  • The highest-value unknown is not a dataset anyone can download: sailings per day and vehicle capacity for the barge, as two separate numbers, held by the operators and absent from every open source the build checked.
  • The tide table for the Dunwich standard port, not obtained, so the tide carries zero constituents and the page shows the marker with a date.
  • Four packs in the inbox, OpenStreetMap, weather, census and ecology, fetched with receipts and waiting on the owner's licence decision.

Putting something in

What a person can put in today.

In the first build, a spoken correction works and has been used: a resident said a business had closed, and the record was closed with the correction attached and the date. A typed-up batch of facts goes through the intake tool, which refuses anything without explicit consent and coarsens any location before it is committed. Whatever is put in arrives as the same shape of record as a council document and passes the same checks; the build's own promise is one spine and no lower-trust lane, your name on the record and nothing else, no points, no badges, no tallies.

In the second build, a person's supplied knowledge is a first-class source. The boat timetables came that way: transcribed by the owner on 19 August 2026, recorded with who said it and when, and sealed so that a later drift in the file is caught by a check and named.

Neither build

Has a form, a portal, an account or a network call.

Contributions arrive out of band and somebody types them in. Both builds write files for a person to read and send on; neither posts anything anywhere by itself. That is the current shape, and both builds describe it in their own documents as the boundary rather than a gap.
