The builds

Three builds, two makers.

A digital twin is a working model of a real place that runs on a computer and can say where each of its numbers came from. Two have been built for Minjerribah, and this site explains them.

This site

The Straddie Twins

Built by Luke with Claude, September 2026. A public companion that explains the two twins part by part, for anyone arriving cold.

Country

Quandamooka Country.

Minjerribah is Quandamooka Country. Both twins acknowledge the Quandamooka People as the Traditional Owners of Minjerribah and of the lands and waters of Moreton Bay, and pay respect to Elders past and present. They are simulations built from public records and carry no cultural material.

This site

It explains the twins and does not change them.

  • Nothing on this site is written into either twin's repository. The twins are read, described and linked, and left as they are.
  • Every screenshot was captured from the running builds on 2 September 2026, with nothing altered.
  • Every count on these pages is computed from one list of parts, kept in the site's own files, so a number on screen is that list and not a figure typed elsewhere.
  • Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No build step, no framework, no tracking, and no network calls beyond the fonts stylesheet.
  • Australian English throughout.

Sources for the imagery

The island pictures on this site are real satellite data.

Every view of Minjerribah here, the moving one on the front page and the photographs behind each heading, is made from open Earth observation data, cut to the island and processed for the screen. Nothing is drawn by hand and nothing is an artist's impression.

Colour

Copernicus Sentinel-2

Scene S2C_56JNQ, 13 July 2026, 0.02 per cent cloud, 10 metres a pixel, true colour. Contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2026, processed by the European Space Agency. Free to use with attribution.

Elevation

Copernicus DEM GLO-30

A global 30 metre elevation model produced for the European Space Agency. It reaches 221 metres on this island against a surveyed high point of 233 metres, which is the smoothing a 30 metre grid gives you. On the front page the heights are raised five times, or the island would read flat over 45 kilometres. Much more than that and a sand island starts to look like a mountain range, which it is not.

Processing

What was done to it

Both layers were cut to the same grid, 1152 by 2644 at 17.1 metres a pixel, covering 19.7 by 45.3 kilometres. The colour had a black point, a gamma lift and a small saturation rise applied, because a July scene at this latitude is dark. No colours were invented and nothing was painted in.

The front page draws the elevation as a surface in the browser and lays the satellite colour over it, then puts the three townships at their real coordinates. Where a browser cannot do that, the same data is shown as a flat relief image instead.

Corrections

If something here is wrong, say so.

Open an issue on this site's repository at github.com/auraofintelligence/straddie-twins. Name the page, quote the line, and if you can, name the file in the twin that says otherwise. A correction to a twin itself belongs on that twin's own repository, linked above.
